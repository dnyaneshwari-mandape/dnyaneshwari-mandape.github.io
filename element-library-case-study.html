01

The Problem

Every enterprise marketing team was pointing generic image models at their brand — and getting brand-shaped noise back.

Generation was fast. Compliance wasn't. Nothing stopped the model from inventing a logo mark, blending two competitors' color palettes, or rendering a mascot with the wrong proportions. Brand teams weren't creating with AI — they were policing it, one re-roll at a time.

  • No shared vocabulary between what "on-brand" meant to a designer and what a prompt string meant to a model.
  • No memory. Every generation started from zero — the model had no concept of "approved," only what it inferred from text.
  • No accountability trail. When a flagged asset shipped anyway, no one could trace which prompt, seed, or seed of a seed caused it.

03

Design Strategy & System Logic

Treat every brand asset like a component, not a picture — and treat the prompt as its build target.

Graphic design gave me the eye for what "on-brand" looks like. Product design meant translating that eye into a machine-readable hierarchy — the same instinct that shaped Atomic Design, applied to a prompt instead of a UI. Nothing generates until it can trace a lineage back to an approved primitive.

01
Primitives
Color tokens, logo lockups, type styles — the atoms. Locked by brand ops, not editable downstream.
02
Elements
Approved components built from primitives — icon sets, textures, photographic treatments.
03
Constraints
Style weights and exclusion rules — what the model is allowed to vary, and what it never touches.
04
Blueprint
The assembled system prompt — every field above compiled into one generation contract.
05
Generation
The model runs inside the blueprint's walls — variation, not invention.
04

06

Reflection

As a graphic designer, I made the asset. As a product designer, I built the thing that decides which assets are allowed to exist — the shift was from authorship to governance.

The hardest habit to unlearn was reaching for taste as the final check. At this scale, taste has to be encoded — into a token, a constraint, a rejection rule — or it doesn't scale past the one file in front of me. The eye still matters; it just moves upstream, into deciding what belongs in the taxonomy in the first place.
