Element Store

Dnyaneshwari Mandape — Product Designer

A modular asset framework that teaches AI generation to compose with reusable design elements — instead of repainting pixels.

Role
Product design, research, prototyping
Domain
AI creative tooling · design systems
Deliverables
Strategy, architecture, hi-fi UI, prototype
TL;DR

AI image tools generate beautiful one-offs, but professional designers don't work in one-offs — they work in systems: reusable assets, brand rules, edits, versions. Element Store is the missing layer between design systems and generative AI. It stores brand elements with semantic meaning, retrieves them by intent, and asks AI to compose graphics from them — so every output is on-brand, editable, and versioned by construction.

For reviewers · This is a concept project. Research was synthesized from 8 practitioner interviews and public community threads; all metrics are stated as targets, not results.
01 — Problem

Generation is a party trick until it survives a second revision.

Watch a marketing designer use an AI image tool for real work and the failure mode is always the same. The first output is impressive. Then the brand review comes back: "wrong blue, logo is distorted, swap the product shot." The tool's only verb is regenerate — and regeneration re-rolls everything, including the parts that were already approved.

The deeper issue: image models output pixels, and pixels have no memory. A generated banner doesn't know it contains your logo, your brand blue, or last quarter's product render. So nothing is reusable, nothing is enforceable, and every asset starts from zero.

No reuse

Designers rarely create from scratch — they assemble from libraries. Generators can't retrieve an existing approved asset; they can only hallucinate a lookalike.

No brand contract

Enterprise value lives in consistency, not novelty. A model with no concept of "brand kit" fails the review loop that actually gates publication.

No editability

Feedback is surgical ("nudge the badge, keep everything else") but regeneration is global. Prompt archaeology replaces direct manipulation.

No retrieval

Teams already lose hours hunting assets across drives and DAMs — then duplicate them anyway. AI is generating more orphaned files, faster.

02 — Research

Eight practitioners, one pattern: AI lives outside the workflow.

I interviewed 8 designers (3 marketing, 2 brand, 2 product, 1 creative director) and tore down 9 tools across two categories — generators (Firefly, Midjourney, Canva Magic Studio, ChatGPT Images, Krea) and asset systems (CC Libraries, Figma components, Canva Brand Kit, DAM tools). Three insights shaped everything downstream.

i.
Generation is used for raw material, never for finals.

7 of 8 used AI weekly — but as a moodboard/ingredient step. Outputs were exported to Figma or Photoshop to be rebuilt with real brand assets. The "last mile" is manual reassembly.

ii.
The unit of trust is the approved asset, not the prompt.

Brand teams sign off on specific elements — a logo lockup, a product render, an illustration style. Anything the model invents must be re-reviewed; anything reused is pre-approved. Reuse is a compliance shortcut, not just a time-saver.

iii.
Prompting is a worse interface than picking.

Participants could describe intent ("summer sale story for IG") but not style parameters. When shown existing elements they chose instantly. Recognition beats recall — Nielsen's oldest heuristic, still undefeated.

What this demonstrates · Framing research around workflow economics (trust, review loops, retrieval cost) rather than feature wishlists — the kind of evidence that changes a roadmap.
03 — Opportunity

An empty quadrant between two crowded ones.

Generators compete on image quality for individuals. Asset systems manage components but can't create. Nobody owns the intersection: system-aware generation. That's infrastructure, not a feature — the same way component libraries became infrastructure for product design.

Generators — Midjourney, Krea, Firefly, ChatGPT Images, Canva Magic (individual, pixel-first)
Asset systems — CC Libraries, Figma components, DAM tools (team-aware, can't generate)
Element Store

Sits in the empty quadrant: system & team-aware and generative — brand-locked composition instead of one-off pixels.

04 — Principles

Four rules that decided every screen.

Reuse before generate

The system always searches the library first. Generation is the fallback for what doesn't exist yet — and its output becomes a new reusable element, closing the loop.

Edit, never regenerate

Every output stays decomposed into its elements. Feedback maps to a single element swap or transform — approved parts are never re-rolled.

Brand as a constraint, not a filter

Brand rules are inputs to composition, not a post-hoc checker. The system can't propose an off-palette layout — violations are prevented, then explained.

Show reasoning, earn trust

Every AI decision — why this element, why this layout — is inspectable and reversible. Confidence is communicated, never performed.

06 — Wireframes & iteration

The first version was wrong in an instructive way.

I ran three lo-fi rounds with 5 of the original participants, testing paper-grade wireframes against the brief "make a sale story." Each round killed a structural assumption before any pixels were spent.

07 — The Store

A library you search by intent, not by filename.

Every uploaded asset is decomposed and embedded: subject, style, palette role, usage history. Search speaks the designer's language — "hero product shot, dark background, holiday" — and results are ranked by semantic match and brand approval status. Recognition over recall, at library scale.

11 — Success

Targets I'd hold this to.

Stated as hypotheses to validate in a pilot, not results — each pairs a user outcome with the business mechanism behind it.

−50%
Production time per asset

Measured brief-to-approved. Mechanism: reuse skips the manual reassembly step research showed is the true bottleneck.

90%+
First-pass brand approval

Share of compositions passing review without changes. Mechanism: constraint-based composition prevents violations upstream.

80%
Element reuse rate

Share of composition content drawn from the library vs freshly generated. The health metric for the whole flywheel.

<1s
Semantic retrieval

Search must feel like recognition, not a query. Above ~1s, designers fall back to scrolling folders and the library decays.

12 — Reflection

What I'd do next, and what I learned.

Next: pilot with one brand team, instrument the reuse rate, and pressure-test the hardest assumption — that a layout engine can compose elements well enough that designers accept it as a starting point rather than fighting it. If that fails, the fallback is narrower but still valuable: brand-aware retrieval inside existing tools.

Learned: the interesting design work in AI tools isn't the generation moment — it's everything around it: trust, provenance, editability, and the review loop. Coming from graphic design, I assumed the craft gap was visual. It's actually systemic: the tools that win will be the ones that respect how design work is reviewed and reused, not just how it's made.

For reviewers · Systems thinking (architecture before screens), evidence-led framing, honest treatment of risk, and interaction craft in the prototype — positioned for AI-tooling and design-platform teams.
